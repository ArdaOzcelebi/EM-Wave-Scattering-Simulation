1
Incident Field (Eᵢ, Hᵢ)
▶

A plane EM wave propagates through free space toward the conducting surface. Electric and magnetic fields are mutually orthogonal and transverse to the wavevector $\mathbf{k}$:

$$\vec{E}_i = E_0 \cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r} - \omega t)\,\hat{e}$$ $$\vec{H}_i = \frac{E_0}{\eta}\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r} - \omega t)\,(\hat{k} \times \hat{e})$$
$\eta = \sqrt{\mu_0/\varepsilon_0} \approx 376.73\,\Omega$ is the characteristic wave impedance of free space. The ratio $|\vec{E}|/|\vec{H}|$ is strictly locked to $\eta$.
2
Induced Surface Current Jₛ
▶

The Lorentz force $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v}\times\vec{B})$ accelerates free conduction electrons. Applying Ampère's law across the boundary collapses the volume current into a surface current sheet:

$$\hat{n} \times (\vec{H}_1 - \vec{H}_2) = \vec{J}_s$$

For an ideal Perfect Electric Conductor (PEC), fields inside vanish identically ($\vec{H}_2 = 0$), yielding:

$$\vec{J}_s = \hat{n} \times \vec{H}_{\text{total}}$$
Current flows strictly perpendicular to both the surface normal $\hat{n}$ and the magnetic field $\vec{H}$. The cross-product naturally enforces this geometric orthogonality.

For real conductors with finite conductivity $\sigma$, fields penetrate to a finite skin depth:

$$\delta = \sqrt{\dfrac{2}{\omega\mu\sigma}}$$
3
Scattered Field (Eₛ, Hₛ)
▶

Each accelerating charge and current element acts as a miniature Hertzian dipole antenna radiating spherical wavelets into space:

$$\vec{E}_s(\mathbf{r}) = -j\omega\mu\iint_S G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\,\vec{J}(\mathbf{r}')\,dS' - \nabla\!\left(\frac{1}{j\omega\varepsilon}\iint_S G\,\nabla'\!\cdot\vec{J}\,dS'\right)$$

Where $G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')$ is the fundamental free-space Green's function:

$$G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}') = \frac{e^{-jk|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}}{4\pi|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|}$$
Total Field = $\vec{E}_i + \vec{E}_s$. On the PEC surface, the tangential component cancels to exactly zero: $\hat{n}\times(\vec{E}_i + \vec{E}_s) = 0$.
BC
Step 1 — EFIE Boundary Condition
▶

On a PEC, tangential $\vec{E}$ must vanish. The scattered field generated by the surface current must cancel the incoming incident field at every point on $S$:

$$\hat{n}\times(\vec{E}_i + \vec{E}_s) = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \hat{n}\times\vec{E}_s = -\hat{n}\times\vec{E}_i$$

Substituting the Green's function integral for $\vec{E}_s$ turns this into the Electric Field Integral Equation (EFIE) with current $\vec{J}$ as the sole unknown.

B
Step 2 — Discretization & RWG Basis
▶

The continuous unknown function $\vec{J}(\mathbf{r})$ is approximated as a finite sum of known basis functions (such as triangular RWG elements) with unknown complex weights $I_n$:

$$\vec{J}(\mathbf{r}) \approx \sum_{n=1}^{N} I_n\,\vec{f}_n(\mathbf{r})$$
This transforms an infinite-dimensional calculus problem into finding $N$ numbers $I_1, I_2, \dots, I_N$.
T
Step 3 — Galerkin Testing → Dense Matrix
▶

Testing against each basis function $\vec{f}_m$ converts the integral equation into the canonical linear system:

$$\mathbf{Z}\,\mathbf{I} = \mathbf{V} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \sum_{n=1}^{N} Z_{mn}\,I_n = V_m$$
$$Z_{mn} = j\omega\mu\iint_S\!\iint_S \vec{f}_m(\mathbf{r})\cdot G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}')\,\vec{f}_n(\mathbf{r}')\,dS'\,dS$$ $$V_m = \iint_S \vec{f}_m(\mathbf{r})\cdot\vec{E}_i(\mathbf{r})\,dS$$

📖 Electromagnetics & MoM Derivation

This laboratory visualizes the fundamental chain of electromagnetic scattering:

1. Incident Wave: Maxwell's equations in source-free space yield the vector Helmholtz wave equation $\nabla^2 \vec{E} + k^2 \vec{E} = 0$, whose fundamental solutions are plane waves $\vec{E}_i(\mathbf{r})$.

2. Boundary Condition on PEC: Tangential electric field components across any interface must satisfy $\hat{n} \times (\vec{E}_1 - \vec{E}_2) = 0$. In a perfect conductor $\vec{E}_2 = 0$, so $\hat{n} \times \vec{E}_{\text{total}} = 0$.

3. Method of Moments (MoM): Solving the Electric Field Integral Equation (EFIE) analytically for arbitrary geometry is impossible. MoM expands $\vec{J} \approx \sum I_n \vec{f}_n$ and uses Galerkin inner-product testing to obtain the matrix equation $\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{I} = \mathbf{V}$.

4. Skin Depth $\delta$: For finite conductivity $\sigma$, the wavevector becomes complex: $\gamma = \alpha + j\beta \approx (1+j)\sqrt{\omega\mu\sigma/2}$. The attenuation factor $\alpha = 1/\delta$ generates decaying eddy currents inside the conductor.
